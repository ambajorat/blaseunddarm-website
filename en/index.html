Why an app at all

Because schedules are the first thing a busy day swallows.

Anyone who catheterises, follows a bladder training plan, or keeps a fixed bowel routine works in intervals. You know them by heart — until a meeting overruns, a train is late, or the day simply gets away from you.

The app does the counting. It speaks up when the interval is over, records what happened, and turns weeks of entries into something you can actually show at an appointment instead of estimating from memory.

  • After a spinal cord injury
  • With multiple sclerosis or spina bifida
  • After prostate surgery
  • With IBS or inflammatory bowel disease
  • During bladder training under medical guidance
  • For family carers who document alongside

Features

Two areas, one log.

Bladder

  • Reminder timerYour own interval, with alerts even on a locked screen.
  • Two-second entryVolume, time, note — straight from the notification or the widget.
  • Urine colourFrom clear to cloudy — one tap, and the report carries it along.
  • Fluid intakeIf you want it: drink volumes in the same log, in and out side by side. Off by default.
  • Quiet hoursThe app stays silent overnight if you set it to.
  • AlertsThe app speaks up when values fall out of range — fixed limits or deviation from your own 14-day average.
  • Catheter supplyFor ISC: counts along with every entry, shows how long your stock lasts and reminds you to reorder in time. Off by default.
  • Early UTI alertsLog signs such as odour, burning or spasticity — the app alerts you immediately for blood or fever and flags patterns across days. Not a diagnosis. Off by default.
  • Palpation checkEstimate bladder filling above the pubic bone in three levels — statistics show your own average volumes per level. Off by default.
  • Autonomic signsLog goosebumps, sweating, feeling hot or headache as filling signals, with an optional blood pressure field. Off by default.

Bowel

  • Bristol scaleTap type 1 to 7, with the explanation right there.
  • Fixed bowel timesIts own rhythm, separate from the bladder timer.
  • HistoryIntervals and irregularities across weeks.
  • NotesFor medication, equipment, or whatever else mattered.

Both

  • StatisticsDaily, weekly and monthly views with averages.
  • Report for your appointmentA PDF with averages, a daily overview and every single entry — plus CSV for your own analysis. Both are generated on the device.
  • ImportRead earlier records back in as CSV, for instance when switching devices.
  • BackupSave and restore all entries without them passing through anyone else’s server.
  • German and EnglishFully translated.

Inside the app

For your appointment

A sheet you can hand over.

From your entries the app produces a report as a PDF: averages at the top, a daily overview below, then every single entry with time, volume, urine colour and Bristol type.

That is the difference between “I think it was more often than usual” and a table you can actually discuss. If you would rather do the maths yourself, take the CSV export.

The report is generated on the device. Where it goes next — printed, emailed, passed on — is up to you.

Usability

Built for hands that do not always cooperate.

The app is meant for people whose fine motor control, strength or sensation may be limited. That shaped the design in several places.

  • VoiceOver reads labels rather than numbers: “300 millilitres”, “Type 4: smooth, soft. Normal”
  • The daily summary is read as one sentence, not a column of figures
  • Tap targets at least 44 points high — Apple’s minimum, usable even when you miss slightly
  • Larger text scales with the system setting without controls disappearing
  • Siri and Shortcuts: log an entry without touching the screen
  • Bristol types shown with symbol and label, not distinguished by colour
  • Dark mode, easy on the eyes at night
  • Readable on the widget, lock screen and Apple CarPlay without opening the app

If something still gets in your way, write to me. Those are the messages that change the most — a good part of what is listed here came about exactly that way.

Your data stays where it belongs.

What you log here is health data. So there is no account, no server, and no analysis on our side. The app stores everything locally on your device — and, if you want, in your own iCloud backup.

  • No sign-up, no email address
  • No trackers, no analytics SDKs
  • No ads, nothing sold on
  • Export and deletion entirely in your hands

Common questions

Do I need an account?
No. The app works right after installation, with no sign-up.
Does anything go into Apple Health?
Not at the moment. A Health connection is planned but deliberately on hold until it works cleanly and read-only.
How do I bring my data to an appointment?
Through the PDF report: averages, a daily overview and every entry on one sheet you can print or email. If you would rather work through the figures yourself, use the CSV export.
What happens when I get a new iPhone?
With iCloud backup on, your entries come across during setup. Without it, export first and import afterwards.
Does the app detect a urinary tract infection?

No. It logs your observations — such as odour, burning or cloudy urine — and flags patterns you should have checked by a doctor. It does not make a diagnosis.

Is there an Android version?
Yes — as a direct download, see Android. Deliberately outside the Play Store.

Who is behind it

Built out of personal need.

The app comes from André M. Bajorat, who writes at ploetzlich-querschnitt.de about everyday life after a spinal cord injury. What went into it did not start on a drawing board but with the question of how to keep track of these times without letting them dominate the day.

Feedback goes straight into the next version — including the small stuff. Just write.

The app is free and will stay that way. If it makes your day easier, you are welcome to say thanks — with a coffee.

A review helps more than you would think.

Hardly anyone talks about this subject, so hardly anyone finds the app. On the App Store, reviews help decide who gets shown it at all. Two sentences are enough.
