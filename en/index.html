How it works

Three steps, a quiet mind.

1

Remind

Your interval or fixed times — the app speaks up, even on the lock screen, in the car, on your wrist. At night it stays quiet if you want it to.

2

Log

One tap on the tile with your volume — saved, timer restarted. If you want to capture more: urine colour, Bristol type, signs, fluid intake, catheter supply.

3

Understand

Statistics across weeks, alerts when values drift out of range — and a PDF report you simply put on the table at your next appointment, instead of guessing from memory.

Bladder

Why an app at all

Because schedules are the first thing to sink in daily life.

If you catheterise, do bladder training or keep a fixed bowel routine, you work with intervals. You know them by heart — until an appointment gets in the way. The app takes over the counting. For:

  • After a spinal cord injury
  • With multiple sclerosis or spina bifida
  • After prostate surgery
  • With IBS or inflammatory bowel disease
  • During bladder training under medical guidance
  • For family carers who document alongside

Where it runs

Runs where your day happens.

iPhone with widget, lock screen and Live Activity · Apple Watch · Apple CarPlay · Siri and Shortcuts · iCloud backup — and Android as a direct download.

Android: direct download, no Play Store.

Deliberately outside the Play Store: the app comes straight from this site. Logging, statistics and the PDF report are the same as on the iPhone; widgets, Apple CarPlay and Siri are missing for obvious reasons.

Two things worth knowing: Android will warn you during installation because the file does not come from the Play Store — that is expected, and you can confirm it. And updates will not arrive automatically; new versions appear here on this page.

The current build covers what matters: Bristol scale, urine colour, catheter supply with reorder reminders, early UTI alerts, palpation check and autonomic signs, stool amount, alerts with suggestions from your data, statistics with CSV and PDF export, editable entries and configurable quiet hours.

Usability

Built for hands that do not always cooperate.

The app is meant for people whose fine motor control, strength or sensation may be limited. That shaped the design in several places.

  • VoiceOver reads labels rather than numbers: “300 millilitres”, “Type 4: smooth, soft. Normal”
  • The daily summary is read as one sentence, not a column of figures
  • Tap targets at least 44 points high — Apple’s minimum, usable even when you miss slightly
  • Larger text scales with the system setting without controls disappearing
  • Siri and Shortcuts: log an entry without touching the screen
  • Bristol types shown with symbol and label, not distinguished by colour
  • Dark mode, easy on the eyes at night
  • Readable on the widget, lock screen and Apple CarPlay without opening the app

If something still gets in your way, write to me. Those are the messages that change the most — a good part of what is listed here came about exactly that way.

Your data stays where it belongs.

What you log here is health data. So there is no account, no server, and no analysis on our side. The app stores everything locally on your device — and, if you want, in your own iCloud backup.

  • No sign-up, no email address
  • No trackers, no analytics SDKs
  • No ads, nothing sold on
  • Export and deletion entirely in your hands

And the app is free — no subscription, no locked features, no ads.

Common questions

Do I need an account?
No. The app works right after installation, with no sign-up.
Does anything go into Apple Health?
Not at the moment. A Health connection is planned but deliberately on hold until it works cleanly and read-only.
How do I bring my data to an appointment?
Through the PDF report: averages, a daily overview and every entry on one sheet you can print or email. If you would rather work through the figures yourself, use the CSV export.
What happens when I get a new iPhone?
With iCloud backup on, your entries come across during setup. Without it, export first and import afterwards.
Does the app detect a urinary tract infection?

No. It logs your observations — such as odour, burning or cloudy urine — and flags patterns you should have checked by a doctor. It does not make a diagnosis.

Is the app available in English?

Yes — fully, since version 4.4: iPhone, Apple Watch, widgets and notifications follow your system language. The PDF report and CSV export deliberately stay in German so existing exports and imports remain compatible. Since version 2.3, the Android app is fully bilingual as well.

Is there an Android version?
Yes — as a direct download, see Android. Deliberately outside the Play Store.

About

Built out of personal need.

The app comes from André M. Bajorat, who writes at ploetzlich-querschnitt.de about everyday life after a spinal cord injury. What went into it did not start on a drawing board but with the question of how to keep track of these times without letting them dominate the day.
